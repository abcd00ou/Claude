## Company Overview

Texas Instruments is the world's largest analog semiconductor company, with a broad portfolio covering analog ICs, embedded processors, and power management. In AI datacenter applications, TI's power management ICs are embedded throughout GPU servers (voltage regulators, gate drivers, current sensing, digital power controllers). TI's most significant AI datacenter move in 2026 was jointly announcing a complete 800 VDC power architecture with NVIDIA at GTC 2026 — a strategic move targeting the next generation of AI datacenter power delivery that transitions from 48V to 800V DC distribution.

**Source:** TI Unveils Complete 800 VDC Power Architecture for Future Generation AI Data Centers with NVIDIA, Texas Instruments Newsroom, 2026-03-16, https://www.ti.com/about-ti/newsroom/news-releases/2026/2026-03-16-ti-unveils-complete-800-vdc-power-architecture-for-future-generation-ai-data-centers-with-nvidia.html

## Supply Chain Position

TI is an IDM (Integrated Device Manufacturer) with its own fabs in Texas, Oregon, and internationally. TI sells analog and power management ICs to AI server OEMs (Super Micro, Dell, HPE, Wiwynn, Quanta), GPU module makers, and hyperscalers directly. TI's 800V architecture partnership with NVIDIA positions TI components as the reference design for the next generation of AI datacenter power infrastructure.

**Key customers (publicly stated):** Major AI server OEMs and ODMs (not named individually in public disclosures), hyperscalers (indirectly through server OEMs)  
**Key suppliers (publicly stated):** TI is self-sufficient for most analog production (own fabs); purchases epitaxial wafers, specialty substrates

**Source:** TI Q1 2026 earnings; TI corporate filings
