## Company Overview

Intel occupies a weakened position in AI accelerators. Gaudi 3 has not achieved meaningful hyperscaler adoption. Falcon Shores was cancelled and replaced by Jaguar Shores as the next-gen AI platform on Intel 18A. Intel Foundry secured its first confirmed external AI design wins in 2026: AWS (custom AI Fabric chips) and Microsoft (Maia 2/Maia 3) both confirmed on Intel 18A. Intel Q1 2026: total revenue $13.6B; DC&AI $5.1B (+22% YoY); Foundry revenue $5.421B with -$2.437B operating loss improving QoQ. Foundry breakeven targeted for 2027.

**Source:** Intel Q1 2026 Earnings Press Release, Intel IR, 2026-04-23; FinancialContent/MarketMinute, 2026-04-08

## Supply Chain Position

Intel is an IDM (integrated device manufacturer) — it designs and manufactures its own chips. Intel Foundry manufactures Gaudi 3 internally. 18A customer chips (external) would be manufactured at Oregon fabs. Intel is not a significant TSMC customer for AI products.

## Updates

### Update: 2024-12-01 — Pat Gelsinger retires; Lip-Bu Tan named CEO in March 2025

> Pat Gelsinger retired as Intel CEO on December 1, 2024 after the board concluded the IDM 2.0 turnaround was not progressing fast enough. Intel had posted a $16.6B loss in its most recent quarter. Lip-Bu Tan, former CEO of Cadence Design Systems, was appointed CEO on March 18, 2025. In 2025, the foundry business was spun off into a wholly-owned independent subsidiary called Intel Foundry, with Microsoft and Amazon (AWS) confirmed as anchor external customers for Intel 18A process.

**Source:** Intel CEO News, Intel Newsroom, 2024-12-01; Intel Appoints Lip-Bu Tan as CEO, Intel Press Release, 2025-03-18

### Update: 2024 — Gaudi 3 launched; $500M revenue target missed; Falcon Shores AI chip cancelled

> Intel launched Gaudi 3 at Intel Vision 2024 in April 2024, claiming 50% better inference performance and 40% better power efficiency vs. NVIDIA H100. Intel guided $500M Gaudi 3 revenue for H2 2024 but officially scrapped the forecast; actual Gaudi revenue was well below target. In January/February 2025, Intel confirmed Falcon Shores (the planned Gaudi 4 generation) was cancelled as a market product. Jaguar Shores was named as the replacement next-generation AI chip on Intel 18A process.

**Source:** Intel Gaudi 3 Launch, Intel Vision 2024; Intel Falcon Shores Cancelled, HPCwire, 2025-02-04

### Update: 2023-12 — Intel 4 HVM and Meteor Lake launch; first 7nm EUV product ships

> Intel launched Meteor Lake (Core Ultra Series 1) in December 2023, the first product manufactured on Intel 4 (7nm-class EUV). Meteor Lake features a modular tile architecture (compute tile on Intel 4, SoC tile on TSMC N6, graphics tile on TSMC N5). The Intel Foundry business recorded FY2023 operating loss of $7.0B on $18.9B revenue — the largest foundry loss in Intel's history. Intel guided for foundry losses to widen further in 2024.

**Source:** Intel Meteor Lake Launch, Intel Newsroom, 2023-12; Intel Foundry $7B Operating Loss, CNBC, 2024-04-02

### Update: 2022-03 — Intel 4 (7nm EUV) and Meteor Lake confirmed; 5-node-in-4-years roadmap

> Intel rebranded its process node naming: Intel 7 (10nm SuperFin), Intel 4 (7nm EUV, formerly Intel 7nm), Intel 3, Intel 20A, Intel 18A. Meteor Lake became the first product targeting Intel 4 process. The 5-node-in-4-years roadmap committed Intel to catching and surpassing TSMC by 2025 on a per-node basis. Intel 4 delivers 20% improvement in clock speed at same power vs. Intel 7.

**Source:** Intel Accelerated Event — IDM 2.0 Roadmap, Intel Newsroom, 2022-07

### Update: 2021-02 — Pat Gelsinger named CEO; IDM 2.0 strategy: open Intel fabs to external customers

> Pat Gelsinger was named CEO of Intel Corporation in February 2021, succeeding Bob Swan. Gelsinger announced the IDM 2.0 strategy (Integrated Device Manufacturing 2.0): continuing to design chips internally while also opening Intel's fabrication facilities to external foundry customers through Intel Foundry Services (IFS). The strategy included plans for new fabs in Arizona and Ohio and €33B investment in Europe.

**Source:** Intel CEO Pat Gelsinger Announcement, Intel Newsroom, 2021-02

### Update: 2024 — Falcon Shores Discrete GPU Cancelled; OAM Module Path Retained

> Intel cancelled the discrete GPU version of Falcon Shores, retaining only the OAM (Open Accelerator Module) form factor compatible with standard AI server chassis. This significantly reduces the product's market reach vs NVIDIA PCIe/SXM form factors.
