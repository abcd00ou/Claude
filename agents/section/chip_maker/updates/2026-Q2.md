### Update: 2026-02-06 — AMD MI450 on TSMC 2nm, 432GB HBM4, 19.6 TB/s; ramp weighted Q4 2026; Meta $60B deal

> AMD MI450 is manufactured on TSMC 2nm (one node ahead of NVIDIA Vera Rubin at 3nm). Memory: 432GB HBM4 vs. 288GB HBM3e on MI350; bandwidth 19.6 TB/s vs. 8 TB/s on MI350 (+145%). Ramp is weighted toward Q4 2026, with small initial shipments in Q3. Meta committed $60B over five years for up to 6GW of MI450 GPUs and receives a warrant for 160M AMD shares vesting at 1GW–6GW shipment milestones.

### Update: 2026-01-09 — Intel ends Gaudi line; Jaguar Shores rack-scale system targets 2026 launch with HBM4

> Intel confirmed no Gaudi 4; Gaudi 3 is the final standalone accelerator. Jaguar Shores replaces Falcon Shores (cancelled January 2025) and integrates compute, networking, and memory in a single rack-scale platform using HBM4 (SK Hynix). Not a discrete GPU — Intel described it as a paradigm shift away from competing head-to-head with NVIDIA and AMD on discrete accelerators.

### Update: 2026-02 — Microsoft deploys world's first large-scale GB300 NVL72 cluster: 4,608 GPUs, 92.1 ExaFLOPS FP4 for OpenAI workloads

> Microsoft deployed the world's first large-scale GB300 NVL72 cluster on Azure: 4,608 GB300 GPUs linked via Quantum-X800 InfiniBand, delivering 92.1 ExaFLOPS of FP4 inference. The cluster is dedicated to OpenAI workloads for multitrillion-parameter model serving. Meta also announced a multi-year AI alliance with NVIDIA centered on GB300 systems, with Meta planning to deploy "millions" of GB300 and Vera Rubin Superchips across gigawatt-scale campuses in 2026 and 2027.
