### Update: 2026-06-08 — Power is the binding constraint: 7 GW delayed/canceled, transformer 160+ weeks, grid queue 2,600 GW; nuclear 9.8 GW committed; DATA Act proposed

> Goldman Sachs: US DC power demand 31 GW (2025) → 41 GW (2026). Of 16 GW announced US AI DC projects for 2026, only 5 GW is under construction; 11 GW stalled (Tech Insider, 2026). At least 7 GW canceled or delayed due to power infrastructure failures — not demand or financing (Tech Insider, 2026). US interconnection queue: 2,600 GW proposed; avg wait 4–7 years in major hubs; ERCOT queue 410 GW with 87% data centers (EnkiAI, 2026). Transformer lead times: standard power transformers 128 weeks avg, generator step-up 144 weeks, custom up to 5 years (Wood Mackenzie / Industrial Sage); switchgear sold out through 2028. PDU: 12–18 month lead times; 10–20% cost inflation from tariffs (RNG Strategy Consulting, 2026). Nuclear response: 13 projects, 9.8 GW committed across hyperscalers; first electrons 2027 earliest. BYOP (bring-your-own-power) model accelerating as developers bypass grid queues. DATA Act 2026: federal legislation proposed permitting off-grid power solutions for data centers (Introl Blog, 2026). State vs. federal tension: multiple US states passing laws restricting large AI DC power connections (MultiState, April 2026).

### Update: 2026-05-11 — U.S. transformer crisis deepens: up to 4–5 year lead times; 50% of 2026 datacenter builds delayed; utilities plan $1.4T capex response

> pv magazine USA (2026-05-11) reports U.S. transformer lead times extending to four years in some segments. Wood Mackenzie projects a 30% power transformer supply deficit and 10% distribution transformer deficit in the U.S. in 2025-2026. Demand in 2026 expected to exceed 2024 levels by 21% for power transformers. Up to 50% of U.S. data centers planned for 2026 are expected to be delayed or canceled (webhosting.today, April 2026); only one-third of the 12 GW planned for 2026 was under construction as of April 2026. U.S. grid interconnection timelines stretch from 5 years to 7 years or more in some regions (enkiai.com, 2026). U.S. utilities plan $1.4 trillion in capital investment tied to AI data center energy demand, representing a 27% capex surge. Hyperscalers are increasingly building on-site dedicated power generation ("energy islands") to bypass public grid interconnection queues. Morgan Stanley models a 126 GW increase in global data center power consumption through 2028 with a projected 49 GW shortfall in the U.S. alone.
