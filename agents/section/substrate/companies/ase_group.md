## Company Overview

ASE Group is the world's largest outsourced semiconductor assembly and test (OSAT) company. Its SPIL (Siliconware Precision Industries) subsidiary is a key TSMC CoWoS packaging partner, receiving approximately 60,000–80,000 CoWoS wafers per year under TSMC's 2026 outsourcing program. ASE is additionally developing CoWoP (Chip-on-Wafer-on-PCB) — its own competing advanced packaging technology — in partnership with NVIDIA. Six new plants are expected to break ground worldwide in 2026.

**Source:** ASE Buys NT$2.8B Zhunan Facility, TrendForce, 2026-01-14, https://www.trendforce.com/news/2026/01/14/news-ase-buys-nt2-8b-zhunan-facility-packaging-orders-reportedly-span-nvidia-amd-and-more/

## Supply Chain Position

ASE is positioned at the back-end of the AI chip supply chain: receives processed wafers from TSMC (via CoWoS outsourcing to SPIL), performs assembly, stacking, and test, then delivers packaged AI accelerator dies. Key customers for AI packaging include NVIDIA, AMD, and Broadcom (per reporting). Intel's EMIB is being evaluated as a back-end alternative by second-tier ASIC vendors as CoWoS capacity remains stretched.

**Key customers (publicly stated):** NVIDIA, AMD (per TrendForce reporting); TSMC (CoWoS outsourcing recipient)  
**Key suppliers (publicly stated):** TSMC (wafer supply), substrate suppliers (FC-BGA), wire bonding materials

**Source:** TrendForce, 2026-01-14; TrendForce, 2026-04-20

## Updates

### Update: 2026-04-20 — CoWoP capacity targeting 20K–25K wafers/month by end-2026; six new plants announced; packaging race intensifies

> ASE Group's CoWoP (Chip-on-Wafer-on-PCB) capacity is projected to reach 20,000–25,000 wafers/month by end of 2026, more than tripling current output. NVIDIA is developing CoWoP packaging technology with SPIL (part of ASE). ASE purchased a NT$2.8 billion Zhunan facility; packaging orders reported to span NVIDIA, AMD, and others; six new plants expected to break ground worldwide in 2026. Intel's EMIB advanced packaging is being evaluated as an alternative back-end option by second-tier ASIC vendors and major U.S. chipmakers as CoWoS capacity remains stretched.
