## Cross-Segment Signals

### Signal 22: Samsung HBM Market Share Collapse — Micron Now #2; HBM Supply Power Shifted

**Segments:** DRAM, Chip Maker, End Market  
**Trigger:** TrendForce Q2 2025 HBM share data; Samsung NVIDIA re-qualification failure

Q2 2025 HBM market share: SK Hynix 62%, **Micron 21%, Samsung 17%**. This is a structural reversal — Samsung was historically #2 at ~35% share. The collapse is traced to Samsung's failure to pass NVIDIA's initial HBM3E qualification for Blackwell production in 2024. Micron's rise to #2 represents the first time Micron has outranked Samsung in HBM. Implications:
- SK Hynix now commands 62% share → 70% projected for NVIDIA Vera Rubin HBM4 (UBS)
- Micron's #2 position gives it structural leverage with both NVIDIA and the hyperscalers
- Samsung's multi-year supply contracts with non-NVIDIA customers provide revenue stability but lock in below-market share
- Samsung's HBM3E re-qualification with NVIDIA — if it succeeds — would reclaim share; if it fails through Blackwell cycle, Samsung risks entering Vera Rubin as a minority HBM4 supplier

#importance:high #confidence:high #cross-ref:dram,chip_maker,end_market

### Signal 23: Vera Rubin 288 GB HBM4 Per GPU — Single Platform Drives $58B→Larger HBM TAM Expansion

**Segments:** DRAM, Chip Maker, Foundry  
**Trigger:** NVIDIA CES 2026 Vera Rubin specs; HBM market forecasts

Vera Rubin R100 GPU requires 288 GB HBM4 per chip (vs. Blackwell B200: 192 GB HBM3E — a 50% increase per GPU). Vera Rubin NVL72 systems deploy 72 GPUs each. A 1,000-node Vera Rubin cluster requires approximately 20.7 TB of HBM4. At estimated $500/stack pricing, the HBM4 cost per NVL72 rack exceeds $2.5M in HBM alone. Cross-segment implications:
- HBM market expansion from ~$35B (2025) → ~$58B (2026) → likely higher with Vera Rubin ramp
- TSMC CoWoS-L required for the larger Vera Rubin package — additional packaging constraint
- HBM4 production concentrated at SK Hynix (~70% projected); Samsung and Micron fight for remaining 30%
- Vera Rubin is on TSMC N3 dual-die, adding to TSMC N3 demand alongside Google TPU and Trainium3

#importance:high #confidence:high #cross-ref:dram,chip_maker,foundry

### Signal 24: Samsung Foundry SF2P at 70% Yield — First Viable AI Chip Competition to TSMC Emerging

**Segments:** Foundry, Chip Maker, ASIC  
**Trigger:** Samsung SF2P 70% yield milestone (January 2026); AMD Venice EPYC negotiations; Preferred Networks AI chip

Samsung SF2P (2nm GAA) reaching 70% yield in January 2026 is the first credible signal that Samsung Foundry may be approaching production readiness at an advanced node for AI applications. Key distinctions:
- Preferred Networks (Japanese AI firm) is already using SF2 for AI chips — first confirmed AI chip at Samsung 2nm
- AMD is in final negotiations for SF2P for "Venice" EPYC server CPUs and "Verano" agentic AI inference chips
- SF2P 70% yield matches the threshold TSMC N3 was at when it entered volume production
- This does NOT yet mean Samsung wins an AI GPU from NVIDIA or AMD for discrete GPU production — but it opens a credible second-source path for EPYC/ASIC workloads
- Intel Foundry 18A remains in sampling with no confirmed high-volume external AI customer

#importance:high #confidence:medium #cross-ref:foundry,chip_maker,asic

### Signal 25: Power Transformer Lead Times at 128 Weeks — AI Data Center Buildout Constrained by Grid, Not Just Chips

**Segments:** DC Infrastructure, End Market, Power  
**Trigger:** Wood Mackenzie data (via TechTicker 2025); U.S. transformer supply shortage data

Power transformer lead times reached 128 weeks (2.5 years) in the United States in 2025, versus weeks in 2020 — a >10× deterioration. Wood Mackenzie reported a 30% supply shortfall for large power transformers in 2025, with demand up 116% since 2019 and 80% of U.S. supply coming from imports. This means:
- AI data center completions are being delayed 24–72 months in some cases by power infrastructure lead times
- Hyperscaler capex commitments ($710–725B CY2026) include projects whose physical completion is years away
- The constraint is **upstream of Vertiv** (whose cooling and UPS equipment are at 12–18 month backlog) — the power delivery to the building is the binding constraint, not the IT equipment inside
- Nuclear, natural gas peaker plants, and utility-scale battery storage are all competing for the same transformer supply
- Vertiv's backlog of $12.45B partly reflects orders placed years in advance to avoid this bottleneck

#importance:high #confidence:high #cross-ref:dc_infra,end_market,power

### Signal 26: Custom Silicon Wave Confirmed — Google Ironwood + Trainium3 Both GA; Four Hyperscalers Now Have Production ASIC

**Segments:** ASIC, End Market, Foundry, DRAM  
**Trigger:** Google Ironwood GA (November 2025); Amazon Trainium3 (December 2025); Meta MTIA Gen 2; Microsoft Maia 200

All four largest hyperscalers now have production AI ASIC programs delivering at scale:
- **Google Ironwood**: GA November 2025; 4,614 FP8 TFLOPS; 192 GB HBM3E; 9,216-chip pod = 42.5 ExaFLOPS
- **Amazon Trainium3**: EC2 Trn3 announced December 2025; 2.52 PFLOPS FP8; 144 GB HBM3e; TSMC N3P; 150K units planned
- **Meta MTIA Gen 2**: 2nm / Broadcom; in production for inference
- **Microsoft Maia 200**: in production on Azure

Cross-segment implications:
- Each of these programs is a TSMC customer (N3/N3P or later nodes) — combined with NVIDIA Blackwell/Vera Rubin, TSMC N3 is now the most contested manufacturing node in semiconductor history
- HBM demand from custom ASICs (Ironwood: HBM3E; Trainium3: HBM3e) adds direct load on SK Hynix/Samsung/Micron outside of NVIDIA GPU requirements
- Custom silicon is not reducing NVIDIA demand — it is additive to total compute demand, creating a rising tide for all supply chain participants
- Trainium4 announced with NVIDIA NVLink Fusion support — the first instance of a custom ASIC explicitly designed to interoperate with NVIDIA's interconnect fabric

#importance:high #confidence:high #cross-ref:asic,end_market,foundry,dram
