### Signal 14: CoreWeave S-1 Reveals Neo-Cloud as Structural Hyperscaler Overflow Layer

CoreWeave's $15.1B committed backlog and $11.9B OpenAI commitment (disclosed in S-1, March 2025) quantifies the neo-cloud as a structural demand layer, not just a cyclical GPU rental market. Microsoft's 35% customer concentration at CoreWeave means Azure is using CoreWeave as overflow capacity — a direct signal that Azure AI demand exceeds what Microsoft can build itself.

**Cross-segment implication:** CoreWeave's "preferred NVIDIA GPU access" means a meaningful portion of NVIDIA's GPU allocation goes through CoreWeave before hyperscalers. This makes CoreWeave's order intake a leading indicator for NVIDIA supply availability. CoreWeave backlog grew faster than hyperscaler capex guidance in Q1 2025, suggesting the neo-cloud tier is absorbing GPU supply that hyperscalers cannot consume fast enough.

→ Cross-ref: `chip_maker/`, `dc_infra/`

### Signal 15: Intel Effectively Excluded From AI Accelerator Market; Gaudi Revenue Not Disclosed

Intel's refusal to disclose Gaudi revenue (stated in Q1 2025 earnings) while reporting flat total revenue (-1% YoY) confirms that Gaudi is not a meaningful contributor. Falcon Shores discrete GPU was cancelled. 18A customer sampling continues but no AI volume win is confirmed. Intel is the only major semiconductor company not participating in the AI accelerator revenue wave.

**Cross-segment implication:** Intel's absence from AI GPU market reinforces NVIDIA-AMD duopoly. Intel Foundry's 18A, if it wins an AI design customer, would be the first credible TSMC alternative for advanced AI chips — but this remains unconfirmed and is a 2027+ event at earliest. The ABF substrate benefit to Ibiden/Unimicron is partially impacted by Intel server CPU volume declining (Intel is a large ABF customer for server CPU packages).

→ Cross-ref: `foundry/`, `substrate/`

### Signal 16: Vertiv Backlog $8.0B and DLC Lead Times Improving — AI Cluster Deployment Pace Accelerating

Vertiv's Q1 2025 orders of $3.3B (+30% YoY) and backlog of $8.0B indicate AI data center construction is accelerating, not decelerating. DLC lead times improved from 40–52 weeks to 28–36 weeks as Vertiv added manufacturing capacity. This is a positive signal for 2026 AI cluster deployment timelines.

**Cross-segment implication:** Shorter DLC lead times mean the power/cooling constraint on AI cluster activation is easing for 2026. This partially resolves the "power as primary constraint" signal from Cycle 1. However, power transformer lead times (52–65 weeks) remain the binding constraint on data center electrical infrastructure — not cooling.

→ Cross-ref: `dc_infra/`, `power/`, `end_market/`
