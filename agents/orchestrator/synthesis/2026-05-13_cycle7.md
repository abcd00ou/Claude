## Cross-Segment Signals

### Signal 33: Samsung HBM Comeback Locks In a Duopoly Structure for Vera Rubin — Micron Squeezed Out

**Segments:** DRAM, Chip Maker, Foundry  
**Trigger:** Samsung HBM3E NVIDIA qualification (Sept 2025); Samsung and SK Hynix confirmed as sole Vera Rubin HBM4 suppliers (March 2026).

After approximately 18 months of failed qualification attempts, Samsung passed NVIDIA's HBM3E qualification in September 2025 and began shipments to NVIDIA in Q3 2025. By March 2026, Samsung and SK Hynix were confirmed as the only two HBM4 suppliers for NVIDIA's Vera Rubin platform (Samsung ~30%, SK Hynix ~70%). Micron was explicitly excluded from Vera Rubin HBM4.

Implication: The HBM supply chain for NVIDIA's highest-priority 2026 platform is now a confirmed duopoly. SK Hynix holds structural dominance. Samsung's recovery is positive for supply security but does not change SK Hynix's pricing position. Micron's exclusion from Vera Rubin is a significant competitive setback — Micron remains relevant for next-generation platforms (HBM4E targeting 2027) and for non-NVIDIA customers.

### Signal 34: GB300 and Vera Rubin Running Simultaneously in H2 2026 — Unprecedented Dual-Platform Pressure on CoWoS and HBM

**Segments:** Chip Maker, DRAM, Foundry, Network  
**Trigger:** GB300 shipping since January 2026 (WCCFTech); Vera Rubin full production ahead of schedule; H2 2026 cloud deployments confirmed at all 8 named providers.

NVIDIA is simultaneously ramping GB300 (Blackwell Ultra, shipping since January 2026, 129% YoY growth projected) and preparing H2 2026 Vera Rubin NVL72 deployments. This creates simultaneous demand on HBM3E (for GB300) and HBM4 (for Vera Rubin), on CoWoS packaging (both platforms require it), and on NVLink fabric infrastructure. Microsoft already deployed the first large-scale GB300 NVL72 cluster (4,608 GPUs) in February 2026.

Implication: The H2 2026 period is the peak demand point for the entire AI supply chain simultaneously across memory, packaging, networking, and power. Any single supply chain element that falls short (CoWoS WPM, HBM4 stack production, transformer availability) creates a bottleneck that affects both platforms.

#importance:high #confidence:high #cross-ref:chip_maker,dram,foundry,network

### Signal 35: Ethernet Has Overtaken InfiniBand for AI Cluster Networking — But NVLink Fusion Repositions NVIDIA Above the Fabric Layer

**Segments:** Network, ASIC, Chip Maker  
**Trigger:** Dell'Oro Group full-year 2025 data; NVLink Fusion ecosystem expansion (Marvell $2B investment, Arm joining, Trainium4 confirmation).

Ethernet now accounts for >2/3 of AI cluster switch sales in FY2025, reversing InfiniBand's 80% share from two years earlier. Oracle has the first confirmed UE 1.0 production deployment. Amazon, Microsoft, Meta, Oracle, and xAI are all named Ethernet users by Dell'Oro Group. Simultaneously, NVIDIA has invested $2B in Marvell and attracted Arm to the NVLink Fusion program — allowing NVIDIA's interconnect to exist *inside* custom ASIC designs rather than competing at the switch layer.

Implication: NVIDIA is not losing the network war — it is moving the battleground. The competition is no longer "InfiniBand vs. Ethernet" but "whose scale-up fabric (NVLink vs. UALink/UEC) becomes the default for heterogeneous AI cluster interconnect." NVIDIA's $2B Marvell bet is its answer.

#importance:high #confidence:high #cross-ref:network,asic,chip_maker

### Signal 36: U.S. Power Infrastructure Is Now the Single Longest Lead-Time Constraint in AI Datacenter Construction

**Segments:** Power, DC Infrastructure, End Market  
**Trigger:** pv magazine USA May 11, 2026 (lead times extending to 4 years); webhosting.today April 2026 (50% DC builds delayed); Wood Mackenzie 30% supply deficit.

Transformer lead times have extended from 128 weeks average to up to 4 years in some segments (as of May 11, 2026). Up to 50% of U.S. data centers planned for 2026 are delayed or cancelled. Only 1/3 of the 12 GW planned for 2026 was under construction as of April 2026. New U.S. transformer manufacturing capacity ($1.8B announced) does not come online before 2027 at best. Grid interconnection queues stretch to 5-7 years in some regions. Hyperscalers are responding by building on-site "energy islands."

Implication: For the first time in the AI infrastructure buildout, the critical path has shifted from semiconductors to civil/electrical infrastructure. GPU supply no longer gates data center deployment — transformer and grid interconnection availability does. This changes the calculus for where hyperscalers build (favoring regions with available power and shorter interconnection queues) and creates a sustained multi-year revenue opportunity for power infrastructure suppliers.

#importance:high #confidence:high #cross-ref:power,dc_infra,end_market

The AMD MI450 deal competes directly with NVIDIA Vera Rubin NVL72 on a GW-for-GW basis for Meta's AI capex in 2026-2027. Meta's TSMC N2 MTIA order adds to N2 demand pressure alongside AMD MI450 (also N2 per prior Cycle 6 data).

### Signal 38: CoWoS Is No Longer TSMC-Exclusive in Volume — Amkor and ASE Now Confirmed in the Supply Chain

**Segments:** Foundry, Substrate, Chip Maker  
**Trigger:** Amkor-TSMC formal MOU; TSMC outsourcing 240K-270K CoWoS wafers/yr to Amkor + SPIL; ASE CoWoP targeting 20K-25K WPM by end 2026.

TSMC has formally outsourced CoWoS packaging at scale to Amkor (~180K-190K wafers/yr) and SPIL/ASE (~60K-80K wafers/yr) in 2026. Amkor and TSMC signed a formal MOU for CoWoS and InFO collaboration in Arizona. ASE's CoWoP technology — with NVIDIA involvement — is targeting 20K-25K WPM by end 2026. Powertech panel-level glass packaging provides a ~30% cheaper alternative to CoWoS-L attracting U.S. AI chipmakers through 2027.

Implication: TSMC's CoWoS pricing power will face incremental competitive pressure beginning in 2027 as OSAT capacity matures. For 2026, TSMC CoWoS remains the bottleneck for leading-edge AI GPU packaging; the outsourced OSAT volume primarily relieves pressure on mid-tier AI chip packaging. The Amkor Arizona facility (production 2028) is the first non-TSMC advanced packaging option on U.S. soil.

#importance:high #confidence:high #cross-ref:foundry,substrate,chip_maker

### Signal 39: $830B in 2026 AI Infrastructure Capex Shifts the Critical Binding Constraint from Capital to Physical Supply Chain

**Segments:** End Market, DC Infrastructure, Power, Foundry  
**Trigger:** TrendForce May 6, 2026 ($830B top-9 CSP capex); all four major U.S. hyperscalers describing demand as capacity-constrained in Q1 2026 earnings.

The top 9 CSPs are collectively spending $830 billion in 2026 on AI infrastructure — a figure larger than most national GDPs. Despite this, Microsoft states Azure demand "exceeds available capacity"; Alphabet describes conditions as "AI demand surges as cloud capacity caps growth"; Amazon's free cash flow collapsed 95% YoY due to investment pace. The binding constraint has shifted from capital availability to physical supply chain: CoWoS packaging slots, HBM stacks, transformers, and grid interconnection timelines.

Implication: Suppliers with scarce, hard-to-replicate assets (TSMC CoWoS capacity, HBM3E/HBM4 production lines, power transformer manufacturing slots, grid interconnection permits) have sustained pricing power through at least 2027. The capital is available; the physical supply chain is not.

#importance:high #confidence:high #cross-ref:end_market,dc_infra,power,foundry
